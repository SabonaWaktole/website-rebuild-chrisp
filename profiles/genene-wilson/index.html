Featured Professional

Genene Wilson

Revenue Architect & Sales Performance Strategist

Finesse Advisers Pty Ltd

Professional Services

Overview

Revenue Architect and Founder of the PROSPER Method helping business owners increase conversions, strengthen leadership identity, and build sustainable revenue through strategic positioning and behavioural sales psychology.

Background

Genene Wilson brings more than three decades of experience in accounting, financial planning, and business advisory, working closely with founders to strengthen profitability, structure, and long-term wealth creation. Her career began in financial services, where she observed that most revenue challenges were not tactical but rooted in behavioural patterns and structural weaknesses within businesses. After successfully selling her financial planning practice, she transitioned into Revenue Architecture and Advisory, combining commercial intelligence with sales psychology and leadership identity development. Genene developed the PROSPER Method to help founders move beyond sales scripts and surface-level tactics toward sustainable revenue growth systems. Today she works primarily with service-based founders and entrepreneurial women, helping them refine positioning, increase conversion performance, and build businesses that are profitable, scalable, and personally aligned.

Interview

Question 1

What inspired you to start this business?

I repeatedly saw capable and hardworking business owners under-earning despite delivering exceptional value. The problem wasn't effort or talent; it was positioning, commercial structure, and confidence in sales conversations. Many founders avoided selling because it felt uncomfortable or misaligned with their identity. I created my business to bridge that gap and help leaders develop both the strategy and the mindset required for sustainable revenue growth.

Question 2

What problem were you determined to solve?

The central issue I saw across industries was low conversion rates driven by poor positioning and inconsistent revenue systems. Many founders rely on sporadic marketing or reactive selling rather than structured revenue architecture. At the same time, internal confidence gaps often undermine strong offers. My work focuses on reframing sales behaviour, strengthening positioning, and building systems that consistently convert opportunities into revenue.

Question 3

How has your vision evolved since launching your advisory practice?

Initially my work focused on improving sales skills and conversion performance. Over time, I realised that sustainable results required a deeper integration of identity, strategy, and commercial structure. This led me to develop a broader framework around Revenue Architecture that aligns leadership identity, positioning, and operational systems. Today the work is far more strategic, helping founders build businesses that are structurally designed to generate sustainable revenue.

Question 4

What leadership principles guide your decisions?

My philosophy is built on a few simple principles: clarity over chaos, structure before scale, ownership before strategy, and results over rhetoric. Founders often try to scale complexity before they establish a clear commercial foundation. I encourage leaders to simplify, take ownership of their positioning, and focus on measurable outcomes. When those fundamentals are in place, growth becomes far more sustainable.

Question 5

What legacy are you aiming to build through your work?

I want to help create a generation of commercially intelligent leaders who build wealth without compromising integrity or personal alignment. Business should be a vehicle for both prosperity and purpose. When founders understand revenue architecture and develop confidence in their commercial decisions, they build stronger businesses that support long-term impact. My goal is to elevate that level of commercial confidence across the entrepreneurial community.
